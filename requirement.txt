FIELDCAPTURE - HANDS-FREE FIELD CAPTURE ON AI GLASSES
=====================================================

WHAT THIS ASSIGNMENT IS (IN SIMPLE WORDS)
-----------------------------------------
Build a mobile app called "FieldCapture" (iOS or Android) using Meta's
Wearables Device Access Toolkit (DAT 1.0) SDK for AI glasses.

A field technician (for example, someone inspecting water damage) walks
through a house wearing smart glasses. They never touch the phone. They
only speak voice commands. The glasses camera takes photos, the app checks
the photo quality, extracts information using AI, and at the end produces
a clean structured job record (JSON) plus a draft list of estimate line
items (what work needs to be done and billed).

You do NOT need real glasses. Use Meta's "Mock Device Kit" instead and feed
it your phone camera or recorded video.

What they really test:
  - How fast you learn an SDK you have never used.
  - How you prototype without the real hardware.
  - Your judgment about what the hardware can and cannot do.
  - Whether the whole loop survives real conditions: bad light, moving
    camera, dropped connection, impatient user wearing gloves.

Docs: wearables.developer.meta.com

Example walk-through:
  - At the water heater:  "capture nameplate"
  - At a stained wall:    "capture damage, kitchen, north wall"
  - Near the sink:        "note: subfloor soft by the cabinet"
  - Walks out -> back office gets job record + evidence photos +
    extracted fields + draft line items.


TIME LIMITS
-----------
- 72 hours from receiving the brief (hard deadline).
- Expected effort: about 16 to 20 hours. Do not burn all 72.


=====================================================
PART 1 - CORE REQUIREMENTS (ALL MUST WORK END TO END)
=====================================================

1. SDK INTEGRATION
   - App registration with Meta DAT.
   - Permissions (camera, mic, bluetooth, etc. as needed).
   - Full session lifecycle on Mock Device Kit:
       * start
       * pause
       * resume
       * stop
   - Recovery when the device disconnects halfway through a walk-through
     (must reconnect / continue without losing data).

2. THREE VOICE COMMANDS
   - "capture nameplate"
   - "capture damage [room] [surface]"   e.g. "capture damage kitchen north wall"
   - "note: [free text]"                 e.g. "note: subfloor soft by the cabinet"
   - If the mock has no audio path from the glasses, fall back to the
     phone microphone AND explain why in the README.

3. CAPTURE QUALITY CHECK (ON DEVICE)
   - Detect bad frames on the phone itself (not in the cloud):
       * blurry
       * too dark
       * badly framed (subject cut off / not in view)
   - If bad, ask the tech to retake BEFORE they move on (voice/audio prompt).
   - Document the thresholds you chose and HOW you arrived at them
     (measured on your own real frames).

4. EXTRACTION (AI / VISION)
   - Nameplate capture -> make, model, serial number, manufacture date.
   - Damage capture   -> room, surface, damage type, rough size.
   - Note             -> converted into structured fields.
   - EVERY field must have a confidence score.
   - Confidence should go DOWN when the answer is wrong (calibrated).

5. JOB RECORD + DRAFT LINE ITEMS
   - Output ONE JSON job record per walk-through, following a schema you
     design yourself.
   - Define your own list of 10 to 15 line-item codes, e.g.
       "remove and replace drywall, per sq ft".
   - Map what was observed to those line items (draft estimate).

6. YOUR OWN EVALUATION
   - Build a test set of AT LEAST 40 frames, including:
       * at least 10 nameplates
       * at least 10 low-light shots
   - Report accuracy PER FIELD (make, model, serial, date, damage type...).
   - Show the 5 worst failures and the cause of each one.

7. BAD CONNECTIVITY / OFFLINE
   - Captures are queued on the device when there is no signal.
   - Upload automatically when signal comes back.
   - Nothing gets lost (survives app restart / disconnects).


=====================================================
PART 2 - STRETCH GOALS (PICK ONLY 1 OR 2, DO THEM WELL)
=====================================================
One done properly scores higher than three half done.

A. SIZE WITHOUT DEPTH
   - Glasses have no depth sensor. Estimate the size of a damaged area
     anyway. Show your error against a real tape measure.

B. GLASSES VS PHONE
   - Capture the same scenes through the mock glasses feed and the phone
     camera. Compare usable-frame rate and extraction accuracy.

C. SENSOR PROTOTYPE
   - Pick one sensor to add to industrial safety glasses (work light,
     time-of-flight, thermal or moisture). Optionally build a bench
     prototype (e.g. ESP32 + ToF sensor). Show data it gives that the
     camera cannot.

D. COST AND LATENCY
   - Measure cost per job and delay from capture to job record.
   - Show how you would cut one of them in half.


=====================================================
PART 3 - DELIVERABLES (WHAT TO SEND)
=====================================================

1. CODE
   - A GitHub repo they can clone.
   - Commit as you go (they read the commit history).

2. README
   - Setup in under 10 minutes on a clean machine.
   - Architecture in ONE diagram.
   - What you would do with two more weeks.
   - Which AI coding tools you used and where.
   - Which vision / speech models you used, why, and cost per job.
   - Assumptions you made where the brief was unclear.
   - Known limitations / what does not work (flagging costs nothing).
   - What you cut and why.

3. DEMO VIDEO
   - Maximum 5 minutes.
   - One full walk-through in your own space.
   - Must include at least one failure the app catches and recovers from
     (e.g. blurry/dark frame retake, disconnect recovery).
   - Footage must come from the exact build you submit.

4. EVAL REPORT
   - The test set (40+ frames).
   - Accuracy per field (real numbers).
   - Five worst failures with their causes.

5. RESEARCH MEMO (ONE PAGE)
   - What today's Meta glasses can and cannot do for field estimation.
   - What you found out about the SDK that the docs leave out.
   - The first hardware change you would make (backed by evidence).
   - Must be your own findings, not a restatement of Meta's docs.

6. SAMPLE OUTPUT
   - Three JSON job records from real walk-throughs.


=====================================================
PART 4 - SCORING (OUT OF 100)
=====================================================
Honest measurements count a lot. A smaller app with real numbers beats a
bigger one that cannot be verified.

  Works end to end ........................ 20
    Voice command -> job record with no hand-holding; recovers from
    disconnects and bad frames.

  Extraction and evaluation ............... 20
    Per-field accuracy, failures explained by cause, confidence scores
    that drop when the answer is wrong.

  SDK depth ............................... 15
    Lifecycle edge cases handled; clear notes on what the mock cannot
    simulate and what will change on real glasses.

  Research and hardware judgment .......... 15
    Memo with your own findings + specific hardware recommendation
    backed by evidence.

  Capture quality engineering ............. 10
    Thresholds measured on real frames; retake prompts a tech would not
    want to switch off.

  Product judgment ........................ 10
    Decisions made for someone wearing gloves in an attic with no signal.
    Explain what you cut and why.

  Code and README ......................... 10
    Readable code, tests where they matter, setup under 10 minutes.


=====================================================
PART 5 - GROUND RULES
=====================================================

ALLOWED:
  - AI coding tools (Meta ships SDK skills for Claude Code, Cursor, etc.).
    Note in README which tools and where.
  - Any vision or speech model. State which, why, and cost per job.
  - Make assumptions where the brief is unclear, and write them down.
    (No questions answered during the 72 hours.)
  - Tell them what does not work.

NOT ALLOWED:
  - Off-the-shelf inspection/estimation products that do the whole job.
    (Libraries and model APIs are fine.)
  - Fake data. Every frame in the test set and demo must be your own
    capture.
  - Filming people, or any property you do not have permission to film.
  - Spending more than $50 on APIs or parts (keep receipts; reimbursed
    up to $50).

INSTANT DISQUALIFICATION:
  - Demo footage that did not come from the submitted build.
  - Accuracy numbers with no test set behind them.
  - Not being able to explain your own code in the live session.


=====================================================
PART 6 - DATA YOU MUST CAPTURE YOURSELF
=====================================================
No data is provided. Film at home or anywhere you have permission.
Suggested scenes:
  - Appliance nameplates (water heater, fridge, washer, AC, microwave...)
    -> at least 10
  - Walls (stains / marks / damage)
  - Ceiling
  - Dark cupboard (low light) -> at least 10 low-light shots total
  - Include blurry / moving-camera shots to test the quality check.


=====================================================
PART 7 - LIVE SESSION (IF SHORTLISTED, 90 MINUTES)
=====================================================
Be ready for:

1. Walk-through (20 min)
   - Run the app live, show architecture, explain your hardest decision.

2. Live change (30 min)
   - Add a 4th voice command end to end while they watch, e.g.
     "capture moisture reading [value] [location]".
   - AI tools allowed; they watch how you direct and verify them.
   - => Design the code so adding a new command is easy.

3. Failure drill (20 min)
   - They pick one of your worst failures. Find root cause and ship a
     measurable fix, or explain why it will not fit in the time.

4. Hardware and research (20 min)
   - What breaks first on real glasses?
   - How would you test it in week one with five techs?
   - What would you ask Meta's developer team?
